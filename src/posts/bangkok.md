I came to Bangkok with a stomach and a notebook and not much else. The notebook came home empty. The stomach did not.

Yaowarat at 9pm is the city compressed into one street: charcoal smoke, chili oil, neon spelling words I can't read but recognize anyway. I ate boat noodles standing up. I ate guay tiew with a spoon held in the wrong hand because my right was sticky. I ate a mango sticky rice that made me close my eyes in public, which is something I usually try not to do.

Our tuk-tuk driver on day two — his name was Aroon — stopped pretending to be just our driver around lunchtime and started showing us his city. He took us to a place his aunt cooked at, hidden behind a hardware store. We did not order; she just brought things. We ate everything. He waved off the tip.

> *In some places you measure the day in monuments. In Bangkok you measure it in meals.*

If you go: arrive hungry. Stay flexible. Tip Aroon if you find him.
