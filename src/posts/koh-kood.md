If Bangkok is a city of meals, Koh Kood is a place of pauses. The boat ride out is long and a little rough, and somewhere in the middle of it the noise of the mainland slips off your shoulders without permission.

I rented a scooter on the first day and forgot it on the second. The island moves at the speed of slow walks. I read in a hammock. I swam at a beach where I was the only person. A waiter at a tiny seafood place laughed at me trying to order in Thai and then taught me three words I have already forgotten.

There are quieter places in the world, surely. But not many that let you stop performing the version of yourself you usually are. I think that's what people really mean when they say *vacation* — not rest, exactly, but permission.

> *I came to Koh Kood with a list of things to do and left with the list still folded in my bag.*

If you go: bring less than you think. Stay longer than you planned. Don't bring your laptop.
